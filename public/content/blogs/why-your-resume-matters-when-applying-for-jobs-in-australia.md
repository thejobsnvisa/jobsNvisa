## Importance of Your Resume 
-----

Looking for work in Australia often begins with one of the most important parts of your job application: a strong resume. This is the document that tells employers about your experience, skills, achievements and qualifications. So having a clear and well-structured resume can help employers quickly assess your suitability for a role. 

If you are an overseas worker applying for Australian jobs, your international experience can be a valuable advantage if you present it in the correct way. However, your resume should also follow Australian recruitment expectations and clearly highlight the skills and experience relevant to the job you are applying for. 

Whether you are a professional skilled worker or a recent graduate, following these tips can help you prepare a resume that meets common Australian recruitment expectations. 

## Keep Your Resume Clear 
-----

Create a resume in a way that employers can easily read and scan it. For that, you can use a professional layout with clear headings, bullet points and a simple, easy-to-read font. Generally, an ideal resume length is two pages, but the exact length depends on your experience and the job you are applying for. 

Avoid using many colours, fancy graphics, long paragraphs or information that doesn’t matter. The goal is to focus on your experience and your skills. 

## Include Your Contact Details and Professional Summary 
-----

Put your name, phone number, professional email address, location and LinkedIn profile link at the top of your resume. Do not include personal details like date of birth, marital status or religion. 

After your contact information, add a professional summary. This should briefly explain your work experience, strengths and what you can bring to the employer. Make sure the summary matches the job you are applying for. Do not use a general statement that could fit anywhere. 

## Highlight Your Skills and Work Experience 
-----

The skills section should include abilities that are relevant to the job. Focus on skills related to the role. Include transferable skills such as communication, teamwork and problem-solving. For your work experience, list your most recent job first, then go backwards. For each job, include your job title, the employer’s name, the location and the dates you worked there. Also include your responsibilities and any achievements that show your impact. 

If you have worked overseas, include that experience. Don’t just list your duties. Instead, highlight results or achievements. Show what you accomplished. 

## Add Your Qualifications and Licences
----- 

List your most recent and relevant qualifications by mentioning their name, institution or training provider and completion date or study period. If applying for the healthcare sector, add details about registration, Australian or overseas healthcare licences, clinical training, short courses, CPR certification and relevant professional memberships as suitable for the role being applied for. 

Keep this section focused on qualifications and credentials that are directly relevant to the job being applied for. 

## Customise Your Resume to Each Job 
-----

Do not send the exact same resume to every employer. Carefully read the job advertisement and determine what skills, qualifications, experience and other requirements the employer is seeking. Then make sure your relevant experience is the thing someone sees on your resume. It is also a good idea to add keywords from the job advertisement so your resume can match what applicant tracking systems are looking for and what the employer needs. 

For example, if an employer is looking for a registered nurse with aged care experience, your resume should clearly highlight your nursing qualification, professional registration, clinical skills and relevant aged care experience.
